A Whole Bank on a Six-Day Clock: Reading the AU Small Finance Bank Leak Claim

ScarlettGroup has put AU Small Finance Bank on a leak-site countdown, claiming customer KYC, credit-bureau data, signed loan documents and staff identity. The claim is unverified - but the scope, and the clock, are worth reading carefully. Tags: data breach, banking, financial services, India, ScarlettGroup, KYC, CIBIL, incident response, RBI, CERT-In, threat intelligence

ScarlettGroup - the same crew behind the recent Rajasthan Police listing - has added AU Small Finance Bank (au.bank.in) to its leak site, with a countdown running roughly six days to publication. No data is out yet. What's out is the actor's inventory of what they claim to hold, and for a bank, that inventory is the part worth reading slowly.

Because this isn't the usual "email + password" dump. Per the listing, the claimed dataset spans the bank's entire operational spine:

Customer KYC (core): names, dates of birth, PAN and Aadhaar, contact and family details, income, education, risk category — plus photos, signatures and KYC-screening records. Credit & financial: CIBIL and FICO reports, bank statements, Aadhaar tokens, bureau data, applicant financials, disbursements and payment schedules. Signed loan documents: eSign PDFs, KFS, sanction letters, NACH mandates, collateral and insurance forms. CRM records: customer contacts, accounts, case notes, work orders, stored files. Staff & internal identity: employee directory, permissions, roles, login history, IP addresses and audit logs — "and much more." Here's why that specific combination is worse than its size suggests, and it's the analysis that matters more than the headline.

This is a full identity-fraud kit, not a mailing list. KYC identifiers (PAN, Aadhaar) plus credit-bureau files (CIBIL/FICO) plus signed loan documents is close to everything an attacker needs for synthetic-identity and loan fraud at scale - not just to impersonate a customer, but to manufacture a plausible financial history and borrow against it. The signatures and eSign PDFs remove one of the last friction points. For victims, this is the kind of data that doesn't expire with a password reset; a leaked Aadhaar–PAN–CIBIL triple follows a person for years.

The staff and audit-log items point inward, not outward. A customer database is stolen data. An employee directory, roles, login history, IP addresses and audit logs is a map of the internal estate — how the bank is wired, who has access to what, and where. That's reconnaissance material, and its presence in the listing suggests the intruder was inside systems, not just a customer-facing table. It also raises the uncomfortable question every defender should ask on a countdown: is the access that produced this still open?

The six-day clock is the whole point. A countdown isn't the breach; it's a deadline, and it's the one window where the defender still has moves. For a regulated bank, that window is also a compliance clock: in India, cyber incidents carry CERT-In reporting obligations (famously within six hours of noticing) and RBI expectations for supervised entities. The organisations that come out of these best treat the countdown as time to act, not time to argue about whether the claim is real.

If this were your incident, the response wouldn't wait for confirmation:

Assume intrusion and hunt for it. Treat the claim as credible until your telemetry says otherwise; scope initial access and lateral movement rather than waiting for a sample to drop. Protect customers now. Prepare for mass-scale identity-fraud risk - tighten step-up verification, watch for loan and account-takeover fraud, and ready customer notifications. Rotate and revoke. Reset staff credentials and sessions; if internal access produced this, that access is burned. Notify, don't improvise. Engage CERT-In / RBI obligations and legal early; a regulated entity's disclosure should be drafted before the timer, not during it. Watch the site and mirrors. Monitor for sample drops so you learn what's actually exposed from your own monitoring — not from the buyer or the press. We're sharing the claimed categories for situational awareness only. No records, credentials, customer data or identifiers are reproduced here — Aadhaar, PAN and CIBIL appear solely as data-class labels. The claim is unverified. But a bank's whole customer file on a six-day clock isn't a story about one listing; it's a reminder that for financial institutions, the countdown is the warning.

Claim-level listing via ScarlettGroup's leak site. Unverified and not independently confirmed at the time of writing.
